# International Retail Sales Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes an international retail transactions dataset using Excel formulas and Pivot Tables. The analysis focuses on revenue generation, transaction volume, average revenue per transaction across countries, and the products generating the highest revenue.

## Data Cleaning Log on Google Sheet

### Step 1: Detected and removed 17 duplicate rows from the dataset.

### Step 2: Identified 8 blank spaces in the description column and filled them with "Unknown Products" to preserve the overall data volume.
	• Formula used: =IF(ISBLANK(D2), "Unknown Product", D2)

### Step 3: Identified and resolved 5 blank entries in the country column by cross-referencing their corresponding Invoice numbers using an XLOOKUP formula to maintain geographical revenue integrity.
	• Formula used: =IF(LEN(TRIM(I2))=0, XLOOKUP(TRIM(A2), A:A, I:I, "Unspecified", 0), TRIM(I2))

### Step 4: Resolved 5 rows of date-time validation errors by switching the file locale from English (United States) to English (United Kingdom). To achieve the desired formatting, the entire column was formatted to date-time, converted to a custom dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm schema, and modified to a 12-hour structure by including AM/PM indicators. The suffixes were then extracted using a text function.
	• Formula used: =LEFT([DateTime], FIND(".", [DateTime]) - 1)

### Step 5: Applied TRIM and PROPER functions to standardize affected inconsistent text values in the description column.

### Step 6: Utilized Find and Replace to standardize and normalize country names across the dataset:
	• Updated RSA to Republic of South Africa
	• Updated 19 instances of EIRE to Ireland
	• Updated USA to United States of America
	• Updated 2 instances of UK to United Kingdom
	• Updated 2 instances of U.K. to United Kingdom
	• Applied TRIM and PROPER functions to ensure absolute consistency across all affected values.

### Step 7: Resolved blank entries in the Customer ID column by cross-referencing matching Invoice numbers using an XLOOKUP formula; any remaining anonymous guest checkouts without an associated account were assigned a placeholder ID of 99999 to preserve total transaction volume.
	• Formula used: =IF(LEN(TRIM(G2))=0, XLOOKUP(TRIM(A2), A:A, G:G, 99999, 0), TRIM(G2))

### Step 8: Dropped 5 rows containing missing values in the price column because they represent critical operational elements and should never be left blank.

### Step 9: Dropped 5 rows containing missing values in the quantity column because they represent critical operational elements and should never be left blank.




## Data Analysis Process

### Step 1: Created a Revenue Column

* Created a new column named `Revenue` by multiplying the quantity of products sold by their unit price.

* Used the structured Excel formula:

  ```excel
  =Table1[Quantity]*Table1[Price]
  ```

* This calculated the revenue generated by each transaction line.

### Step 2: Analyzed Total Revenue by Country

* Used the `SUMIF` function to calculate the total revenue generated by each country.

  ```excel
  =SUMIF(Table1[Country],A3,Table1[Revenue])
  ```

* **Key findings:**

  * The United Kingdom dominated the dataset, generating total revenue of **17,057.19**.
  * Denmark ranked second with **1,083.60**, followed by Ireland with **1,010.00**.
  * The Netherlands generated **872.13**, while France generated **735.76**.
  * The United States recorded the lowest reported country revenue at **11.80**.

* The results showed a substantial difference in revenue contribution between the United Kingdom and the other countries.

### Step 3: Counted Transactions by Country

* Used the `COUNTIF` function to calculate the number of transactions associated with each country.

  ```excel
  =COUNTIF(Table1[Country],A31)
  ```

* **Key findings:**

  * The United Kingdom recorded the highest transaction count at **893**.
  * Germany followed with **20 transactions**.
  * Several countries recorded only one transaction each.
  * The results indicated that transaction activity was heavily concentrated in the United Kingdom.

### Step 4: Calculated Average Revenue per Transaction by Country

* Used the `AVERAGEIF` function to calculate the average revenue associated with each country.

  ```excel
  =AVERAGEIF(Table1[Country],A59,Table1[Revenue])
  ```

* **Key findings:**

  * Denmark recorded the highest average revenue per transaction at **541.80**.
  * The Netherlands ranked second with **174.43**.
  * The United States and unspecified regions recorded some of the lowest average revenue values, below **12.00**.
  * Although the United Kingdom generated the highest total revenue, Denmark achieved the highest average revenue per transaction.

* This analysis helped distinguish countries with high overall revenue from those with higher average transaction values.

## Key Insights

1. **Geographic concentration:** The United Kingdom was the dominant market in terms of both total revenue and transaction volume.
2. **Average transaction value:** Denmark achieved the highest average revenue per transaction despite ranking below the United Kingdom in total revenue.
3. **Revenue distribution:** The revenue figures varied substantially across countries, suggesting that sales activity was unevenly distributed.
4. **Product performance:** Assorted Farmyard Animals In Bucket and Gold Wine Glass were the two highest-revenue products in the product analysis.
5. **Complementary analytical methods:** Excel formulas and Pivot Tables provided alternative ways to summarize and validate the sales data.

## Conclusion

The analysis revealed that the United Kingdom was the leading country by total revenue and transaction count, while Denmark had the highest average revenue per transaction. At the product level, Assorted Farmyard Animals In Bucket generated the highest revenue. These findings demonstrate how spreadsheet formulas and Pivot Tables can be used to summarize sales performance and identify important patterns in an international retail dataset.
